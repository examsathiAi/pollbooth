PULSE
Public Sentiment & Polling Platform
Product Vision, Feature Documentation & Technical Blueprint
Version 1.0 — Final
1. Vision Statement
Pulse is a platform where the public becomes the voice of the country — a place where people vote on
curated questions covering government policy, civic issues, Bollywood, sports, and regional matters; share
why they feel that way privately; see their opinions recognized publicly through agree/disagree reactions;
and follow live results the way they’d follow a news feed.
The platform is designed to make people feel heard without exposing them to bullying, harassment, or
anonymous targeting — every safety decision in this document exists because an earlier, less safe version
of this idea was considered and rejected during design discussions.
Pulse is positioned as both a sentiment/survey research tool and a public awareness platform — an
‘eye opener’ that surfaces what people across the country actually think about government decisions,
entertainment, and current events, in a format people want to return to daily.
2. Core Design Principles (Non-Negotiable)
• No anonymous private messaging between users, ever — this removes the harassment/
grooming surface that sank comparable apps (Sarahah, Yolo, LMK, Kik).
• No user-to-user direct targeting — questions are always admin-authored, never “ask a specific
person” or “rate a specific person.”
• No profile visits, no reply button, no DMs on opinions — public opinions can only be
agreed/disagreed with, never replied to.
• No paywall on content already directed at a specific user (e.g., no “pay to see who said
this”) — this pattern caused the FTC settlement against NGL and is permanently excluded.
• No selling of personal/individual data — only aggregated, anonymized analysis is sold.
• All monetization gates control the paying user’s own visibility, capacity, or aesthetics
— never someone else’s private content.
• No private chat infrastructure — eliminating CSA risk, moderation nightmares, and app-store
rejection vectors entirely.

3.3 Targeted Poll Distribution Engine
Admins do not just “publish” a poll. They target it to specific user cohorts, and the poll appears directly
on those users’ home feeds.
Admin Creation Flow:
• Question, options, category, duration.
• Audience Filters: Age, Gender, State, City Tier, Income, Education, Employment, Vehicle Ownership.
• Estimated Reach calculated in real-time based on active user base matching filters.
• Publish → System auto-assigns poll to matching users’ feeds.
User Feed Logic:
• Polls appear in the “For You” feed if the user matches the targeting criteria.
• Organic (non-commercial) polls prioritized above sponsored polls.
• Expired polls auto-archive but remain viewable in Category Hubs.

3.5 Advanced Opinion Interaction Rules
Opinion Display:
• Opinions shown in a threaded list below poll results.
• Sort options: Top (by agree count), Newest, Controversial (high agree + high disagree).
• Each opinion card shows: anonymous badge, demographic hint (e.g., “Gen Z, Mumbai”), text,
agree/disagree counts.
Strictly Prohibited Interactions:
• No reply button.
• No “share this opinion” button (share the poll result, not the individual opinion).
• No username click / avatar tap → no profile navigation.
• No “message user” option.
Allowed Interactions:
• Agree / Disagree buttons.
• Report button (flag icon).
• Similar Opinions grouping (algorithmic clustering).
Community Moderation:
• 3 reports on any opinion → auto-hide pending human review.
• User can block another user → never sees that user’s opinions again (but both can still vote).

4.3 Additional Safety Measures
• Internal review of aggregate private “reason” text before any internal team reads it in

• Human review queue for admin-authored question content itself — since Pulse writes all

inflammatory framing before publication.
• Severe violations (threats, hate speech) → immediate permanent ban + account flag
— zero-tolerance track for content that represents genuine safety/legal risk.

7. Phased Rollout Plan
Phase 1 — Launch (Months 1–2)
• Core voting loop: admin-authored polls, preset options, private reason field, public opinions with
agree/disagree only.
• General-interest, all-India categories only: Bollywood, sports, current events, light civic topics.
• Guest voting via shareable link; phone OTP signup for full participation.
• Progressive profiling system live.
• Targeted poll distribution engine live.
• Ads as the sole monetization channel.
• Three-layer moderation live from day one.
• Badge system active.
• Goal: prove engagement and retention via the news-feed mechanic before adding higher-risk features.
Phase 2 — Civic & Regional Expansion (Months 3–5)
• Location-based polls and the civic issue-reporting/escalation feature.
• Survey suggestion pipeline.
• Referral-driven growth mechanics.
• Begin approaching local businesses for sponsored/promoted poll pilots.
• Introduce methodology-footer transparency on all published results.
• Paid survey referrals (non-medical only).
Phase 3 — B2B & Research Revenue (Months 6–12)
• Demographic-targeted B2B polling product, once user base supports meaningful cohort sizes.
• Aggregated data reports sold to research/ad agencies.
• Premium subscription launch.
• Legal review specifically for: political/election-adjacent polling processes, and paid third-party
research referrals (especially any health-related studies) before either goes live.
Phase 4 — Political & Sensitive-Category Polling (Only after dedicated legal sign-off)
• Political sentiment polling, with ECI-announcement-linked auto-disable already built and tested.
• Any medical/health-related paid survey referrals, only after confirming whether Ethics Committeelevel review is required for the specific study design.

9. Summary
Pulse is designed around one governing rule: nothing in the product should let one user weaponize
anonymity or paid access against another identifiable person. Every feature in this document — adminonly questions, no reply/DM/profile-visit capability, private-then-optional-public opinion flow, layered
moderation, consent-gated data use, progressive profiling, targeted distribution, and phased rollout of
higher-risk categories — exists because of a specific risk identified and deliberately designed against
during planning.
What remains is standard, well-precedented product and business execution: a news-feed-style engagement loop, transparent methodology, and a monetization stack that sells analysis rather than people.
Pulse is not a survey tool. It is the front page of public opinion for India.

3. Database Operations: The Rules That Prevent Production Disasters
3.1 Migrations: Non-Negotiable
Rule: Every schema change is a versioned, reversible migration file. Never hand-edit production schema.
Never.
# Prisma workflow (recommended)
npx prisma migrate dev --name add_moderation_actions_table
npx prisma migrate deploy # Run in production CI/CD
npx prisma migrate status # Verify before deploy
Migration naming convention:
• YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS_descriptive_name.sql (if raw SQL)
• add_user_moderation_status (if Prisma/TypeORM)
Pre-deploy checklist:
• Migration tested on copy of production data (staging)
• Migration is backward-compatible (no destructive column drops during deploy)
• Rollback script tested
• Migration runtime estimated (< 5 seconds for tables > 1M rows)

7.2 Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)
Every major technical decision is recorded as a short, dated markdown file:
# ADR 003: Why Modular Monolith (Not Microservices)
Date: 2026-07-15
Status: Accepted
## Context
Considered microservices for "scalability" but team is 2 engineers.
## Decision
Use modular monolith (NestJS modules) with clear boundaries.
Extract to microservices only when a module has independent deploy needs.
## Consequences
- Simpler local dev, testing, deployment
- Can still scale horizontally (stateless API behind load balancer)
- Extraction path clear when revenue justifies dedicated team

13. Summary
This appendix transforms Pulse from a well-documented idea into a production-executable system.
Every item here is derived from real startup experience, adapted for bootstrapped constraints:
• No premature scale (no Kubernetes, no microservices)
